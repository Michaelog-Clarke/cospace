## E - What are the most common problems or complaints you hear from colleagues, team leads, or admins about hybrid working?
- Time is lost through commuting to and from the office.
- It can be difficult to locate or connect with colleagues when working across different locations.
- Some employees feel shy or hesitant about coming into the office.
- New team members can feel uncertain about how to approach colleagues or integrate into the team.
- Communication can become inconsistent when people are not physically present together.
- Employees may struggle to build informal working relationships and trust in a hybrid setting.

## E - What impact do those issues have on productivity, morale, or trust in the process and how do you usually solve it?
- There is no clear solution currently.
- Many employees feel shy or less confident when interacting with colleagues, which can negatively affect workplace relationships.
- It becomes more difficult to get to know team members and ask questions, resulting in slower communication and collaboration.
- Work processes can take longer because people are not as comfortable engaging with their teams.
- A lack of confidence in in-person interactions can reduce morale and weaken trust in the hybrid working process.
- Improved communication tools, clearer office expectations, and stronger team onboarding could help address these issues.

## M - What security, privacy, or legal concerns do you have around storing and managing booking data, especially in relation to employee personal data, access control, auditability, and who should be allowed to see or change bookings?
- Security and data protection policies must comply with GDPR requirements.
- Additional training on data protection and security practices would be beneficial.
- The current database is perceived as not being particularly secure.
- Employees should be able to see when resources are booked but not necessarily who made the booking unless ofcourse it is their own.
- Administrators should have enhanced access, while users should only be able to view and manage their own bookings.
- Access control should be role-based to protect sensitive booking information.
- Booking records should be auditable so changes can be tracked and reviewed when needed.
- Clear ownership of data access and editing rights is important to maintain privacy and accountability.

## T - How do managers currently monitor attendance, team collaboration, and workload, and what challenges do you anticipate when employees are working in different locations?
- Administrators use the current system to monitor who is attending the office and when.
- Managers can view attendance information for their own teams.
- Team cohesion can be more challenging in a hybrid environment.
- Employees may be less confident or more reserved, which can slow communication and collaboration.
- Hybrid working allows organisations to recruit talent from a wider geographical area, creating both advantages and challenges.
- Managers may find it harder to assess workload and team engagement without regular in-person visibility.
- Attendance data should be used to support planning and team management rather than simply monitoring people.

## S - How do exceptions get handled today, for example last-minute cancellations, no-shows, double bookings, visitors, leadership requests, or accessibility needs, and which of those scenarios create the biggest operational risk?
- Most exceptions are handled manually.
- Last-minute cancellations and no-shows can cause frustration, waste resources, and increase administrative effort.
- Double bookings require manual intervention to resolve.
- Accessibility requirements are a key consideration and can present operational challenges if not accommodated properly.
- Visitor requests and leadership exceptions may require quick decision-making and flexible room allocation.
- The highest operational risk is likely to come from missed bookings, double-booking, and inaccessible spaces not being managed effectively.
- A clear escalation process would help reduce disruption when exceptions occur.

### Accessibility considerations:
- Support for employees with mobility requirements.
- Availability of screen reader functionality and other accessibility features.
- Priority booking for client-specific rooms or specialised spaces where required.
- Accessible room layouts and clear booking information for all staff.
- Consideration of individual needs when allocating spaces or managing exceptions.
- Ensuring accessibility is built into the booking process rather than added as an afterthought.
